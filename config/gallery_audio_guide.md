# Gallery Audio Guide Knowledge

Use this as official knowledge about what is inside CGGallery, Antarctica.
Do not read it verbatim. Answer briefly in the visitor's language.
For entrance mode, use it only when the visitor asks what is inside.
For guide mode, use it as the main exhibition knowledge.

## Guide response rules

- Keep normal answers to 2-5 short sentences.
- Use one room, stand, artwork, or fact per answer unless the visitor asks for an overview.
- If the visitor asks for a full explanation, offer a short guided sequence and ask whether to continue.
- When speaking from the entrance, say "inside" or "in the room", not "in front of you".
- When speaking in guide mode with a hall or stand in the URL, assume the visitor is near that room or stand.
- Do not invent prices, discounts, measurements, species details, cruise terms, or services not listed here or in venue config.
- If a detail is unknown, send the visitor to Natalia or the administrator.

## Overview

CGGallery, Antarctica is an immersive photographic exhibition by Sergey Potetyunin, photographer and traveller.
It is built as a journey to Antarctica: departure, ice and light, animals, then a final room with photo zone, short film, virtual reality, works for sale, souvenirs, and conservation message.
The exhibition should feel like an expedition, not just a room with pictures.

## Hall 1 - Beginning of the Journey

Main themes:
- Sergey Potetyunin is the photographer and author of the exhibition.
- He was born in Omsk, Siberia.
- After a spinal injury and many years of pain, he recovered through yoga and martial arts, then travelled to the Himalayas and Antarctica.
- The Antarctic expedition began in February 2024 in Ushuaia, the southernmost port, at the edge of Tierra del Fuego.
- The route crossed the Drake Passage, where Pacific and Atlantic waters meet.
- The Drake Passage is presented as a psychological border: a test before entering another world.
- The first iceberg at sunrise marks the emotional moment when one world ends and another begins.

Stands and works:
- Stands 1 and 2: information about Sergey and the expedition route.
- Stand 3: triptych "Sun and Ice", an Antarctic sunset landscape; three flying birds can be seen against the largest iceberg.
- Near the entrance: large photograph "Purple Dawn"; it looks almost three-dimensional, but it is real Antarctic light, not graphics.

Discovery story:
- Antarctica was discovered only about two hundred years ago, and different countries still dispute who saw it first.
- In 1820 Bellingshausen saw the icy edge of unknown land.
- Three days later Bransfield saw the Antarctic Peninsula.
- John Davis, a seal hunter, may have been the first to step ashore.
- There is also an older Spanish trace: in 1603 Gabriel de Castilla may have reached Antarctic waters.
- In 1819 the Spanish ship San Telmo disappeared near the South Shetland Islands with 644 people aboard; if survivors reached land, they may have been the first people on Antarctic soil.
- Spain has two Antarctic research stations: Juan Carlos I and Gabriel de Castilla.

Safe phrasing:
- Use "according to one version", "may have", "probably", or "the exhibition tells this as a historical mystery" for disputed discovery facts.

## Hall 2 - World of Ice and Light

Main themes:
- Ice is the main character here, not just a background.
- Icebergs are born from ancient glaciers that slowly move from the continent toward the ocean.
- When huge masses break off and enter the sea, their new life begins.
- Very old iceberg ice can look dark blue because it is dense and contains few air bubbles.
- Around ninety percent of an iceberg is hidden underwater.
- Antarctica is beautiful and fragile; the exhibition connects wonder with climate awareness.

Stands and works:
- Stand 4: iceberg forms; visitors are invited to imagine shapes in the ice.
- The guide names shapes such as "Guardian of Antarctica", an icy swan, an old mushroom or tired giant, and penguins sliding from icy hills.
- Stand 5: "Ice Crocodile", showing how strange ice forms can become.

Official climate/ice facts from the audio guide:
- The Antarctic ice sheet contains most of the planet's fresh water in frozen form.
- The audio guide says the ice sheet contains about ninety percent of the planet's fresh water.
- The audio guide says if it melted, sea level would rise by about seventy meters.
- The audio guide says Antarctica loses about 150 billion tons of ice per year.
- Use these numbers only when useful; do not overload casual visitors.

## Special Stand - Falkland Islands and South Georgia

Placement:
- This stand is between the second and third rooms.
- It is about the second expedition, 2025-2026.

Falkland Islands / Las Malvinas:
- Before reaching Antarctica, the expedition stopped at the Falkland Islands.
- In Argentina they are called Las Malvinas.
- The 1982 war between Argentina and Britain is part of the islands' history.
- The exhibition notes that nature gradually becomes stronger than the political story.
- The islands are windy, open, green and treeless, with grasslands reaching the ocean.
- They are home to many seabirds, including penguins, albatrosses and cormorants.

South Georgia:
- South Georgia felt like a natural miracle.
- It was once a center of whaling; commercial whaling caused severe declines in whale populations.
- After the international ban on commercial whaling, whale populations began recovering.
- Around South Georgia it is now possible to observe humpback whales, fin whales, minke whales and other Southern Ocean giants.
- The audio guide mentions a whale tail photograph about four meters wide.
- A whale is a mammal, not a fish: it breathes air, gives birth to live young, and feeds them milk.
- The audio guide says a blue whale can reach twenty-five meters and weigh one hundred twenty tons.

Restoration and animals:
- South Georgia ran a major project to remove invasive rats and mice that destroyed bird eggs and chicks.
- The project cost around ten million pounds.
- In 2018 South Georgia was declared rat-free, and native bird populations began recovering.
- People and animals coexist unusually closely there.
- Fur seals and elephant seals can rest near beaches, paths and buildings without fear.
- The key message is restoration: after past damage, nature can recover if people protect it.

## Hall 3 - The Hosts

Main themes:
- In Antarctica, humans are not the main characters.
- Animals often show curiosity rather than fear because Antarctica entered human history recently.
- The exhibition teaches respect: observe, do not touch.
- International agreements prohibit touching wild Antarctic animals.
- Respect begins when a person stops treating touch as a right.
- Foreign microbes can also affect local flora and fauna.

Animals:
- Weddell seal: presented as calm but powerful.
- The audio guide says it can be up to three and a half meters long, dive to eight hundred meters, and remain underwater for about an hour.
- It chews breathing holes in ice; old seals' teeth can be worn down by this work.
- Antarctic blue-eyed cormorant: the audio guide says it can dive to one hundred sixteen meters.
- Wandering albatross: the audio guide says its wingspan reaches three and a half meters and it can fly for twenty days without stopping.

Penguin section:
- "If Antarctica had a face, it would be the face of a penguin."
- Penguins first seem funny because of their formal black-and-white look and short steps, but they are highly adapted.
- Stand 7: a large photo of a gentoo penguin with wings open and head raised.
- Gentoo penguin: third largest after emperor and king penguins.
- The audio guide says males can weigh up to nine kilograms and reach ninety centimeters.
- Stand 6: photo group showing gentoo penguins in motion.
- The audio guide says gentoo penguins can reach thirty-six kilometers per hour in water, making them the fastest penguins.
- The audio guide says they can dive to two hundred meters.
- Their wings are stiff flippers, their bones are denser than many birds, and their legs are set back: awkward on land, excellent underwater.
- Penguins stay warm with dense waterproof feathers, down, body fat, circulation adaptations in wings and feet, and salt glands that help them drink seawater.

Penguin family and installation:
- Stand 8: family photographs.
- Named photo moments include "Maternal Warmth", "Children home, parents happy!", "Had a walk!", chicks arguing with father, "Three Friends", and a funny moment when everyone scratches their sides at once.
- There is a playful central story: a chick asks why humans are photographing them, and the father says humans may want to show people their world.
- There is an "Icarus of the penguin world": a penguin repeatedly climbs a rock, runs and jumps, trying to fly, but cannot.
- The installation shows a gentoo penguin nest: father penguin, chick still covered with down, mother away for food.
- The chick has no real feathers yet, no air layer, and cannot swim; if it falls into water, it may die.
- The emotional message: in wild nature tenderness and vulnerability go together.

## Hall 4 - Touch

Main themes:
- Final room with Antarctic landscapes, transparent air and unique light.
- Stands 9 and 10: Antarctic landscapes.
- The author felt each of these frames was one of the best moments of his life.

Visitor experiences:
- In the center is a photo zone: visitors can photograph themselves on board a liner near Antarctica.
- In the far corner is a porthole; opening it reveals a penguin family with a view of the bay.
- In front of the video screen there is a place to sit and watch a short film about the expedition.
- Virtual reality is available for deeper immersion, especially the kayak journey around icebergs. On first mention, explain that visitors put on a headset and enter an Antarctic scene.
- Extra virtual reality episode prices must be checked with the administrator / Natalia.

Products and partners:
- Works can be purchased and taken home.
- Any photograph from the catalogue can be produced on premium equipment; the catalogue is on the tablet near the exit.
- CGGallery is a partner of the French cruise company PONANT; visitors interested in seeing Antarctica in person can ask the administrator.
- The souvenir corner includes author photographs, photo souvenirs and memorable gifts, many made in the gallery's workshop.
- Children can find soft toys and Antarctic characters.
- Souvenir purchases support WWF, helping protect nature, climate and wild species.

Closing message:
- The exhibition was not created only to show beautiful photographs.
- Its purpose is to help visitors feel a connection with a world that exists without humans, beautiful and fragile.
- The most important journeys end when a new way of seeing stays with you.

## Fast answers for guide mode

If asked "what is inside?":
Answer with four rooms: journey start, ice/light, animals/penguins, final room with photo zone, film, virtual reality, and souvenirs.

If asked "where should I start?":
Tell them to begin with Hall 1 and move slowly; the exhibition is built like an expedition.

If asked "what should kids see?":
Mention penguins in Hall 3, the nest installation, photo zone, porthole, virtual reality, and soft toys.

If asked "what is the best photo spot?":
Mention the Hall 4 photo zone and the porthole with penguin family; inside photography is allowed according to venue policy.

If asked "what is the most emotional part?":
Mention the gentoo penguin nest and the chick's vulnerability, or the first iceberg moment in Hall 1.

If asked "what is the most surprising fact?":
Pick one: most of an iceberg is underwater, Antarctica holds most fresh water in ice, or gentoo penguins are extremely fast underwater.
